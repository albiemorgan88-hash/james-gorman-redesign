Pupil Voice Wellbeing Survey: Turning Feedback Into Action is a practical guide for pupil voice leads, pastoral teams, senior leaders and school council coordinators.

The focus is collecting safe, useful feedback from pupils and turning it into visible school action. HIP Psychology supports schools across Northern Ireland and Ireland with pupil workshops, staff training, parent sessions and whole-school wellbeing planning.

Why this topic matters

Pupil voice can become tokenistic if schools gather feedback but do not close the loop. A simple wellbeing survey should ask questions pupils can answer safely and should lead to actions they can recognise.

What schools should decide before delivery

  • What pupils can realistically influence
  • How anonymity and safeguarding are handled
  • Who analyses responses
  • How actions are reported back

Practical activities that can help

  • Question design
  • Theme sorting
  • You-said-we-did planning
  • Pupil council follow-up

How staff can follow up afterwards

Staff can use survey themes to plan assemblies, form-time activities, parent sessions or targeted workshops for year groups that need extra support.

Where this fits in a whole-school approach

This topic can sit within a wellbeing calendar, pastoral care policy, school development plan or staff training programme. The aim is to make support visible before concerns become harder to manage.

FAQs

What should pupil voice wellbeing survey include?

It should include a clear purpose, age-appropriate examples, safe boundaries, practical activities, staff follow-up and signposting routes for pupils, families or staff who need more support.

How can schools keep this work safe?

Use scenarios rather than personal disclosure, brief staff before sensitive topics, keep safeguarding routes clear and avoid asking pupils to share private experiences in front of peers.
