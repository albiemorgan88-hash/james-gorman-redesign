Wellbeing Day for Schools: Planning a Useful Pupil and Staff Programme is a practical guide for principals, SLT, pastoral leaders and wellbeing coordinators.

The focus is designing a focused wellbeing day that has a purpose beyond one-off activities. HIP Psychology supports schools across Northern Ireland and Ireland with pupil workshops, staff training, parent sessions and whole-school wellbeing planning.

Why this topic matters

A wellbeing day is strongest when it feeds into a wider school plan rather than sitting as a standalone event. Schools need wellbeing input that pupils can understand, staff can follow up and leaders can connect to pastoral and safeguarding systems.

Key planning decisions

  • What problem the day is meant to support
  • Which pupils and staff need separate inputs
  • How safeguarding and follow-up will work
  • What pupils should remember a week later

Practical activities schools can use

  • Year-group workshops
  • Staff training input
  • Parent evening
  • Whole-school reflection activity

How staff can follow up afterwards

Staff should know what to notice, what to record, what language to use and when to escalate. A strong session leaves adults with repeatable prompts they can use in normal school time.

Where this fits in a whole-school plan

This topic can sit inside a wider wellbeing calendar, pastoral care policy, school development plan or staff training programme. The aim is to make support visible before problems become harder to manage.

FAQs

What should a wellbeing day for schools session include?

It should include a clear purpose, safe boundaries, age-appropriate examples, practical activities, staff follow-up and signposting routes for pupils or families who need more support.

How can schools keep wellbeing sessions safe?

Use scenarios rather than real incidents, avoid public disclosure, brief staff before sensitive topics and make safeguarding or pastoral routes clear at the end.
