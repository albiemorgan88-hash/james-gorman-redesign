# Press Pitch: The Wellbeing Layer Schools Are Missing in AI Rollout

**Working headline:** AI in schools is not just a technology rollout. It is a staff wellbeing and pupil trust issue.

**Angle:** As schools consider how AI will affect planning, homework, assessment, safeguarding and workload, the missing conversation is the human layer: teacher confidence, pupil anxiety, trust, boundaries and change management.

**Why HIP Psychology:** HIP Psychology works with schools on wellbeing, behaviour, learning and performance. The team is well placed to comment on how leaders can introduce AI without leaving staff to absorb the uncertainty alone.

**Suggested comment piece:**

Schools do not need another abstract argument about whether AI is good or bad. They need a practical conversation about how adults and pupils experience change. Staff may be curious, sceptical, anxious or already experimenting quietly. Pupils may see AI as a shortcut, a threat, a creative tool or another source of comparison. Leaders need to hold all of that while still making clear decisions.

The wellbeing layer of AI rollout should cover four questions:

1. What do staff need to feel confident rather than exposed?
2. How will pupils be taught boundaries, trust and help-seeking around AI?
3. How will safeguarding and pastoral teams respond when AI creates new forms of harm or pressure?
4. How will leaders communicate change without adding another invisible workload to teachers?

AI training can explain tools. Schools also need support with the psychology of adoption: uncertainty, professional confidence, pupil safety and sustainable routines.

**Potential outlets:** education press, school leadership newsletters, EA-adjacent commentary, local NI education media.
