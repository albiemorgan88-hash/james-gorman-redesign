# What Does Under Offer Mean in Northern Ireland?

Under offer usually means that a seller has accepted an offer on the property, but the sale has not reached the binding contract stage. The buyer still has mortgage, survey and legal work to complete, and the seller still has documents and enquiries to deal with.

The wording used by agents and property portals can vary. In Northern Ireland, the important point is that an accepted offer is not the same as an exchanged contract or completed sale.

## Is under offer the same as sale agreed?

The two terms are often used for a similar stage. A buyer has made an offer that the seller intends to proceed with, and the property may be removed from active marketing or shown with a status label.

Neither label transfers ownership. The solicitors still need to work through title, searches, enquiries and the contract. A mortgage buyer also needs the lender to complete its assessment and issue the required offer.

## Can I still view a property that is under offer?

Ask the estate agent. The seller decides whether viewings continue, and the answer can depend on how far the accepted buyer has progressed. Some sellers pause viewings while others keep a record of interested buyers in case the transaction stops.

An agent should give you an accurate status and should not create a false impression that a property is freely available. If viewings are no longer being arranged, you can ask to be contacted if the position changes.

Keep searching in the meantime. A property can return to the market, but there is no guarantee that it will.

## Can another buyer make an offer?

An estate agent must pass offers to the seller, unless the seller has given a lawful written instruction that changes how offers should be handled. The seller decides whether to consider another offer and should take their solicitor's advice about their position.

A buyer should be honest about their mortgage, deposit, chain and timescale. Do not describe yourself as a cash buyer or chain-free unless that is accurate. A clear, proceedable offer is more useful than a larger figure with an uncertain route to completion.

## What should the accepted buyer do next?

Instruct a Northern Ireland solicitor, progress the mortgage application and arrange an appropriate survey. Reply promptly when the agent, lender or solicitor asks for information, but use verified channels for financial and identity documents.

The lender's valuation is for the lender. It does not replace a buyer's survey. The legal work and property checks can also raise questions that were not visible during a viewing.

The buyer should keep the agent updated on genuine progress. If a delay appears, explain it early rather than leaving the seller to guess.

## What should the seller do next?

Instruct your solicitor and return the property paperwork promptly. Gather planning approvals, Building Control documents, guarantees, certificates and information about any mortgage secured on the property.

Keep the home in the agreed condition and tell your solicitor about anything that changes. The estate agent can help the parties stay informed, but legal questions belong with the solicitors.
