# What Are Title Deeds in Northern Ireland?

Title deeds are the documents and records used to show ownership of land and the rights, restrictions and mortgages that affect it. In Northern Ireland, the way title is proved depends on whether the property is registered in the Land Registry or recorded through the Registry of Deeds system.

Most buyers and sellers do not need to interpret title documents themselves. Their solicitors obtain and review the relevant records, prepare the contract papers and explain any issue that needs a decision.

## What information can title documents contain?

The title can identify the owner and the land included in the sale. It may record rights of way, access, shared arrangements, restrictive covenants, leases, mortgages and other matters that benefit or burden the property.

Plans are read in their legal context and should not be treated as a precise site measurement without professional advice. A fence or hedge on the ground may not answer a legal boundary question by itself.

If you have a concern about access, parking, a shared lane, an extension near a boundary or land used with the house, tell your solicitor early.

## What is the difference between Land Registry and Registry of Deeds?

Land Registry records the ownership of registered land. The Registry of Deeds provides a system for recording deeds affecting unregistered land. The appropriate checks and documents depend on the property's title system.

nidirect explains that the Registry of Deeds does not guarantee that a recorded deed is valid or has the legal effect claimed. That is one reason legal review matters.

Your solicitor will know which registry is relevant, obtain the required records and explain how ownership will be transferred through the sale.

## Do I need paper deeds if I have a mortgage?

Do not assume that no paper file exists simply because title information is registered electronically. The lender, solicitor or previous owner may hold documents that are still useful for planning, covenants, leases, rights, guarantees or the property's history.

If you are selling, ask your solicitor what is needed. Do not send original documents to an unverified address or discard old property papers before they have been reviewed.

Where a mortgage is secured on the home, the seller's solicitor normally obtains the redemption information and deals with releasing the lender's security from the sale proceeds.

## What should a Derry seller gather?

Contact your solicitor early and provide any title papers you hold. Also gather planning approvals, Building Control completion information, guarantees, certificates, lease or management papers and documents for extensions or alterations.

Tell the solicitor about changes to access, boundaries, names, ownership or borrowing. Missing documents do not always prevent a sale, but finding the issue before a buyer is waiting gives the professionals more time to deal with it properly.

## What should a buyer ask about the title?

Explain how you intend to use the property and ask your solicitor about anything important to that plan. Examples include parking, access, a home business, an extension, shared areas or future alterations.

Read the solicitor's report and ask questions before signing a contract. An estate agent can help with factual property and transaction updates, but cannot give the buyer legal advice on the title.
