# Derry Property Market Update — Spring 2026

The Derry property market has started 2026 with real momentum. Buyer demand is strong, stock levels remain tight, and average prices across the city continue to edge upwards. Whether you're thinking of selling, buying, or simply keeping an eye on the market, here's what's happening on the ground right now.

## Average Prices Continue to Rise

Property prices in Derry have been on a steady upward trajectory over the past 18 months, and spring 2026 is no exception. The average house price in the Derry City and Strabane council area has climbed noticeably, driven by a combination of limited supply, strong demand, and improving buyer confidence.

Three-bedroom semi-detached homes — the bread and butter of the Derry market — are seeing particularly strong demand. A well-presented three-bed semi in popular areas like Culmore, Waterside, or the Cityside is attracting multiple viewings within days of listing, and in many cases, offers above asking price.

Detached family homes in desirable locations are also performing well, with buyers competing for limited stock. At the other end of the market, apartments and smaller terraced properties continue to offer excellent value, particularly for first-time buyers and investors.

## Demand Outstripping Supply

The single biggest story in the Derry market right now is the imbalance between supply and demand. Put simply, there aren't enough homes on the market to satisfy the number of active buyers.

At **James Gorman Property**, we're seeing more registered buyers than available properties across almost every price bracket. Homes that are well-presented and realistically priced are selling quickly — often within the first two weeks of going live.

This is good news if you're a seller. It means you're in a strong negotiating position, viewings are plentiful, and the chances of achieving full asking price (or above) are higher than they've been in years.

For buyers, it means being prepared is essential. Having your mortgage agreement in principle ready, your solicitor on standby, and being able to move quickly when the right property comes up can make the difference between securing your dream home and missing out.

## Waterside Regeneration Driving Interest

The Waterside continues to be one of the most exciting areas in Derry from a property perspective. Ongoing regeneration projects have transformed parts of the east bank, and buyers are increasingly recognising the value on offer.

Properties in the Waterside consistently offer more space for your money compared to equivalent Cityside locations. A three-bedroom semi that might cost £160,000–£180,000 on the Cityside can often be found for £130,000–£150,000 in well-connected Waterside areas.

The improved infrastructure, new retail and leisure facilities, and better transport links are all contributing to a shift in perception. Areas that were once overlooked are now actively sought after, and we expect this trend to continue through 2026 and beyond.

## Ebrington: The Area Everyone's Watching

If there's one postcode in Derry generating the most buzz right now, it's Ebrington. The transformation of the former military barracks into a vibrant mixed-use quarter has been remarkable, and its effect on surrounding property values is clear.

The combination of waterfront living, cultural venues, restaurants, and proximity to the city centre makes Ebrington hugely appealing to young professionals and downsizers alike. Properties in and around the Ebrington area are commanding premium prices, and demand shows no sign of slowing.

New developments in the area are being snapped up quickly, and existing properties nearby are benefiting from the halo effect. If you own property in the Ebrington vicinity, your investment has likely appreciated significantly over the past two years.

## Fort George: New Builds on the Horizon

The Fort George site represents one of the most significant development opportunities in Northern Ireland. Located on the banks of the River Foyle, this former military base is set to be transformed into a major mixed-use development incorporating residential, commercial, and leisure elements.

While large-scale construction is still in its planning and early development phases, the anticipation is already influencing the wider market. The prospect of hundreds of new homes, along with associated infrastructure improvements, is generating significant interest from developers and buyers alike.

New build properties are particularly popular with first-time buyers taking advantage of schemes like Help to Buy NI, and the Fort George development is expected to provide much-needed additional housing stock for the city.

## What's Driving the Market?

Several factors are combining to create the current market conditions:

### Mortgage Rates Stabilising

After the volatility of previous years, mortgage rates have settled into a more predictable pattern. While they remain higher than the historic lows of 2021, rates are competitive enough to keep buyers active. Fixed-rate deals are popular, with many buyers locking in for five years.

### Remote Working

The lasting legacy of the pandemic continues to influence property decisions. Many buyers are prioritising space — both indoor and outdoor — over proximity to traditional employment centres. This is benefiting areas like Eglinton, Claudy, and Dungiven, where buyers can get significantly more for their money while still being within easy commuting distance of Derry.

### Population Growth

Derry's population is growing, driven by a combination of natural growth, returning diaspora, and people relocating from more expensive parts of the UK and Ireland. This additional demand is putting further pressure on an already tight housing supply.

### Limited New Build Supply

While there are new developments in the pipeline, the rate of new build completions hasn't kept pace with demand. Planning delays, construction costs, and labour shortages have all contributed to a slower-than-expected delivery of new homes.
