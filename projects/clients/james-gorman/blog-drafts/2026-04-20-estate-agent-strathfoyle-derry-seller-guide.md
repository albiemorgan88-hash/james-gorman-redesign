## Pricing a Strathfoyle home sensibly

Sellers often know their home too well. That can make pricing harder.

You may remember every improvement, every cost and every reason the property feels valuable. Buyers look at it differently. They compare your home against what else they can buy this month and what work they may need to do after moving in.

That is why pricing should be grounded in the local market.

A sensible valuation should account for:

- exact position in Strathfoyle
- overall condition and standard of finish
- whether kitchens, bathrooms and décor feel current enough
- parking, privacy and garden usefulness
- how the house compares with other homes buyers are likely to view

A realistic price gives the listing a better opening. That early credibility matters.

## What to improve before putting the property on the market

The strongest pre-launch work is usually simple, visible and practical.

That often means:

- repainting tired or heavily marked areas
- clearing excess furniture so rooms look more useful and better proportioned
- sorting obvious snagging that creates buyer doubt
- tidying driveways, boundaries and front entrances
- making the garden look usable and maintained

These are not glamorous jobs, but they often make the biggest difference to how the property feels online and in person.

## Why the launch window matters so much

The first week or two on the market is when the property gets its cleanest shot. Buyers are most curious then. The home feels new. If the price and visuals are right, viewings usually come earlier and feedback is stronger.

If the launch is weak, that momentum can disappear.

The most common mistakes are:

- pricing too hopefully
- listing before the home is visually ready
- using poor photos that flatten the rooms
- writing copy that says very little about who the property suits

A better launch gives the listing a stronger chance of being taken seriously from the start.
