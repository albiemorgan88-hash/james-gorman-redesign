## Intro draft
A lot of AI training fails for a very simple reason. It teaches tools before it teaches roles, risks, and daily use cases.

Employees do not need another generic presentation about how AI will change the world. They need to know what it helps with in their job, where they can use it safely, what needs review, and how the business expects AI-assisted work to be handled.

That is what practical AI training should do. It should increase useful adoption, reduce avoidable risk, and give managers a clearer operating standard instead of a vague sense that the team has been “trained”.

## Suggested structure

### H2: What good AI training for employees should cover
- role-specific use cases
- workflow examples, not abstract demos
- prompt hygiene and critical review
- data handling and policy boundaries
- when to escalate to a manager or specialist

### H2: Why one-size-fits-all training usually fails
- different needs for leadership, admin, ops, support, sales, and technical staff
- over-focus on tools instead of process
- no follow-up, no adoption measurement
- no tie back to policy or quality control

### H2: The four layers of a sensible training programme
1. **Awareness:** what AI can and cannot do.  
2. **Role-based application:** real examples for each team.  
3. **Risk and governance:** data, policy, review, compliance.  
4. **Adoption support:** office hours, templates, repeat sessions, and measurement.

### H2: What managers should measure after training
- how many people are actually using the tools
- what tasks are being accelerated
- whether output quality is improving or slipping
- whether policy breaches or risky behaviours are appearing
- whether more advanced support or workflow design is needed next

### H2: When to bring in external help
- the business needs a policy and training package together
- teams are experimenting with AI unevenly or unsafely
- leadership wants adoption but internal capability is weak
- the next step is workflow automation or OpenClaw rollout

## FAQ ideas
1. What should AI training for employees include?  
   Role-specific use cases, safe usage rules, review habits, and clear policy boundaries.
2. How long should employee AI training last?  
   It depends on the team, but shorter focused sessions with follow-up usually outperform one large generic workshop.
3. Do all staff need the same training?  
   No. Sales, ops, finance, and leadership need different examples and different guardrails.
4. How do we know the training worked?  
   Measure adoption, output quality, and whether teams are using AI in the workflows it was intended to support.
5. Should policy come before training?  
   They should be designed together. Training without policy creates risk. Policy without training creates confusion.
