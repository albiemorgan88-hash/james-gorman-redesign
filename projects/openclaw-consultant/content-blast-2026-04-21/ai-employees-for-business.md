## Intro draft
Most businesses do not actually want “an AI employee”. They want fewer dropped tasks, faster response times, less repetitive admin, and a cleaner way to keep routine work moving without hiring too early.

That is why the phrase is useful. It points to a real buyer need. But the best deployments are rarely a magical replacement for a member of staff. They are usually a scoped workflow, or a small group of agents, that handles a narrow but repetitive part of the job well.

If buyers understand that distinction early, they make better decisions. They stop shopping for AI theatre and start looking for practical operating leverage.

## Suggested structure

### H2: What businesses usually mean by an AI employee
- A digital worker is normally a combination of instructions, tools, memory, approvals, and reporting.
- It is best suited to repeatable work, not undefined leadership or relationship-heavy work.
- Good examples: inbox triage, lead qualification, meeting prep, recurring reporting, customer follow-up, document handling.

### H2: Where AI employees save the most time
- Sales admin and first-response work.
- Customer support triage and routine replies.
- Operations checks, reminders, and exception handling.
- Finance admin like invoice routing and reconciliation prep.
- Team support tasks like note summarising and weekly reporting.

### H2: Where humans still matter
- final approvals
- judgement calls with financial, legal, or reputational risk
- complex client communication
- handling edge cases and conflict
- defining priorities and success measures

### H2: The safest rollout model for SMEs
- Start with one workflow, not “replace a role”.
- Give the business one internal owner.
- Define what the AI can do without approval and what must be checked.
- Measure time saved, response speed, error reduction, or revenue impact.
- Move from assistive mode to more autonomous execution only after trust is earned.

### H2: What buyers should ask before they spend
- What exact workflow is this replacing or improving?
- What tools or channels does it need to touch?
- Where does human review stay in place?
- What is the cost of drift or mistakes?
- Is this a one-off build, a managed service, or an internal capability we need to own?

## FAQ ideas
1. Are AI employees the same as chatbots?  
   No. A chatbot mainly answers questions. An AI employee usually needs to complete work across tools, memory, approvals, and recurring tasks.
2. Can AI employees replace staff completely?  
   Usually no. They replace slices of repetitive work. Good teams keep humans on judgement, escalation, and ownership.
3. What is the first workflow to automate?  
   Usually the one that is high-frequency, rule-based, and painful enough that improvement is easy to measure.
4. Do SMEs need enterprise software first?  
   Usually no. Most should start with one workflow and the lightest stack that can run it well.
5. How should buyers measure ROI?  
   Time saved, faster response, fewer dropped tasks, better consistency, or lower support/admin cost.
