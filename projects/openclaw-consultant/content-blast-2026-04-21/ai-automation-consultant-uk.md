## Intro draft
Most businesses do not hire an AI automation consultant because they want more ideas. They hire one because something in the workflow is still too manual, too slow, too inconsistent, or too dependent on somebody chasing work by hand.

That means a good consultant should not just talk about AI. They should clarify the process, narrow the first use case, define where human approval stays, and help the business get to a live workflow without turning the project into an expensive fog of possibilities.

The easiest way to spot weak consulting is simple. If the proposal sounds exciting but vague, the delivery will usually feel the same.

## Suggested structure

### H2: What an AI automation consultant should actually do
- audit workflow friction
- identify the right first automation opportunity
- map tools, channels, data, and approvals
- deliver a pilot or implementation path that is measurable
- document the operating model so the business is not left guessing

### H2: What a serious delivery plan usually includes
- discovery and process review
- workflow design and tooling choice
- pilot build or implementation phase
- training and handover
- optional managed support after go-live

### H2: The questions buyers should ask before hiring
- What would you automate first and why?
- Where would human review stay in place?
- What results would you expect in the first 30 to 60 days?
- What happens after launch?
- What should we not automate yet?

### H2: Consultant vs in-house team
- when an internal ops or technical team can own it
- when outside delivery is faster and safer
- when the best option is a mixed model, internal owner plus external specialist

### H2: How pricing should be discussed
- assessment / discovery work
- pilot delivery
- implementation work
- support retainers
- why buyers should avoid unclear all-inclusive promises

## FAQ ideas
1. What does an AI automation consultant do?  
   They help design and deliver AI-enabled workflows, not just choose tools.
2. When should a business hire one?  
   Usually when the workflow crosses tools, departments, or approvals and the cost of getting it wrong is real.
3. Is this different from AI strategy consulting?  
   Yes. Strategy explains direction. Automation consulting should get specific about workflow execution.
4. How long does a project usually take?  
   Small pilots can move in weeks. Broader rollouts take longer, especially where approvals or integrations matter.
5. Do we still need an internal owner?  
   Yes. Every automation needs somebody inside the business to own priorities and sign-offs.
