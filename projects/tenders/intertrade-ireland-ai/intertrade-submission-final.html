3. AC-1: Approach to Analysing the Current AI Capability Landscape (10%)

Overview

Blue Canvas AI will deliver a comprehensive mapping of the AI capability landscape across both jurisdictions, synthesising existing research, identifying gaps, and ensuring InterTradeIreland's work adds unique all-island value without duplicating existing studies.

Methodology

Phase 1: Systematic Literature and Policy Review (Weeks 1–3)

We will conduct a structured desk review of all significant AI strategies, research, and initiatives across both jurisdictions, including but not limited to:

  • Northern Ireland: AI for NI Strategic Overview; AICC Baseline Study; DfE 10X Economy strategy; Invest NI innovation programmes; Ulster University/QUB AI research outputs
  • Ireland: Ireland's AI: Here for Good strategy; Enterprise Ireland AI programmes; IDA Ireland technology sector analysis; SFI research centre outputs (Insight, Lero, CeADAR)
  • Cross-border/International: InterTradeIreland's Corporate Plan 2023–25; EU AI Act implications; UK AI Safety Institute outputs; OECD AI Policy Observatory data
  • Government Policy: Programmes for Government in both jurisdictions; relevant Departmental strategies (DETE, DfE)

Each source will be catalogued in a research matrix documenting: scope, methodology, key findings, SME relevance, and gaps identified. This ensures full traceability and avoids duplication.

Phase 2: Stakeholder Mapping and Consultation (Weeks 2–5)

We will identify and engage key stakeholders across the AI ecosystem in both jurisdictions:

  • Enterprise agencies: Invest NI, Enterprise Ireland, Local Enterprise Offices, InterTradeIreland programme teams
  • Industry bodies: AICC (AI Collaboration Centre), Technology Ireland, Digital Catapult NI, Catalyst
  • SME representative bodies: FSB, ISME, Chambers of Commerce (cross-border)
  • Academic/research: CeADAR, Ulster University AI Centre, QUB ECIT, ATU, DCU
  • Government policy teams: DfE Digital Economy Unit, DETE AI policy team

Engagement methods:

  • Semi-structured interviews (15–20 key stakeholders) exploring perceptions of landscape maturity, gaps, and opportunities
  • Online survey distributed via enterprise agency networks targeting 200+ SMEs across both jurisdictions, capturing current AI awareness, usage, and perceived barriers
  • Two facilitated roundtables — one in each jurisdiction — bringing together 8–12 stakeholders to validate preliminary findings

Phase 3: Gap Analysis and Value Mapping (Weeks 4–6)

Using the combined desk research and stakeholder input, we will produce:

  1. AI Landscape Map: A visual and narrative overview of all current strategies, initiatives, supports, and research across both jurisdictions — identifying overlaps, gaps, and areas of fragmentation
  2. Regulatory Analysis: Assessment of the dual UK/EU regulatory environment (including the EU AI Act vs UK's pro-innovation approach) and its specific implications for cross-border SMEs
  3. InterTradeIreland Value Proposition: Clear identification of where InterTradeIreland can add unique value through all-island approaches — specifically areas where neither jurisdiction is acting alone and cross-border coordination would benefit SMEs

Quality Assurance

  • All findings triangulated across multiple sources (desk research, quantitative survey, qualitative interviews)
  • Research matrix shared with InterTradeIreland steering group at Week 3 checkpoint for early validation
  • Draft landscape analysis presented at interim milestone for feedback before integration into final report

Key Differentiator

As a practitioner working daily with SMEs adopting AI across Northern Ireland, Blue Canvas brings ground-level insight that purely academic or consultancy-led approaches miss. We know which existing programmes SMEs are actually aware of, which they find useful, and where the real gaps lie — not just the gaps visible from a policy perspective, but the gaps experienced by businesses on the ground.

Deliverable

A comprehensive AI Capability Landscape chapter forming Part 1 of the final report, including the visual landscape map, regulatory analysis, stakeholder consultation summary, and gap analysis with clear identification of InterTradeIreland's unique value-add opportunities.

4. AC-2: Evaluating SME Productivity and Competitiveness Gains from AI (10%)

Overview

Blue Canvas AI will place SME productivity at the core of this research, delivering an evidence-based assessment of how AI and wider digital adoption can drive measurable improvements for SMEs across the island of Ireland — distinguishing clearly between superficial experimentation and transformational adoption.

Methodology

2.1 Defining the Productivity Framework (Weeks 2–3)

We will establish a clear analytical framework distinguishing three tiers of AI adoption:

TierDescriptionExampleProductivity Impact
ExperimentationAd hoc use of free/low-cost AI tools by individualsStaff using ChatGPT for drafting emailsMarginal; no process change
IntegrationAI tools embedded into specific business processesAutomated customer service chatbot; AI-assisted bookkeepingModerate; measurable time/cost savings
TransformationAI fundamentally reshaping business model or value propositionAI-driven product development; predictive supply chain managementSignificant; competitive advantage

This framework allows InterTradeIreland to move beyond simplistic "do you use AI?" metrics toward meaningful assessment of adoption depth and impact.

2.2 Sector-Specific Opportunity Analysis (Weeks 3–6)

We will analyse AI productivity potential across sectors most relevant to the all-island SME economy:

  • Manufacturing/Engineering: Predictive maintenance, quality control automation, supply chain optimisation — particularly relevant given strong cross-border manufacturing supply chains
  • Agri-food: Precision agriculture, traceability systems, demand forecasting — a critical all-island sector with significant AI potential
  • Professional Services: Document automation, client analytics, compliance monitoring — high SME density in both jurisdictions
  • Retail/Hospitality: Customer personalisation, inventory management, dynamic pricing — tourism and hospitality as cross-border growth sectors
  • Construction: BIM integration, project management automation, health & safety monitoring
  • Creative/Digital: Content generation, design automation, data analytics — growing sector in both Derry-Donegal and Dublin corridors

For each sector we will assess: current adoption levels (from survey data and existing research); realistic productivity gains based on international evidence and case studies; specific AI applications most relevant to SME scale and budget; and whether existing support interventions are adequately tailored to sectoral needs.

2.3 Quantitative and Qualitative Evidence Gathering

SME Survey (200+ respondents):

  • Current digital maturity baseline
  • AI tools in use and adoption tier (per framework above)
  • Perceived productivity impacts (time saved, revenue impact, cost reduction)
  • Barriers to deeper adoption
  • Awareness of available supports

In-depth Case Studies (8–10 SMEs):

  • Select SMEs across both jurisdictions and multiple sectors demonstrating different adoption tiers
  • Document specific AI implementations, costs, outcomes, and lessons learned
  • These become powerful evidence for the final report — real stories from real businesses

International Benchmarking:

  • Compare all-island SME AI adoption rates against comparable small economies (Denmark, Estonia, New Zealand, Scotland)
  • Identify what leading jurisdictions do differently in supporting SME AI adoption
  • Source data from OECD Digital Economy Outlook, EU DESI Index, and national statistics offices

2.4 Assessing Current Interventions

We will evaluate whether existing enterprise support programmes adequately address SME AI adoption needs:

  • Invest NI's innovation support (Technical Development Incentive, Innovation Vouchers)
  • Enterprise Ireland's Digital Transition programme and AI offerings
  • InterTradeIreland's existing programmes (Fusion, ACSONI, Trade programmes)
  • Local Enterprise Office supports in Ireland
  • Academic-industry partnerships (CeADAR, Catalyst, university programmes)

Assessment criteria: accessibility for SMEs, depth of AI-specific content, cross-border availability, and alignment with actual SME needs identified through our primary research.

Key Differentiator

Blue Canvas works with SMEs implementing AI every day. We see first-hand which tools deliver real productivity gains and which are hype. Our assessment will be grounded in practical experience — we can distinguish between an SME that's genuinely transformed its operations with AI and one that's simply bought a ChatGPT subscription. This practitioner perspective ensures the research goes "behind generic process improvements" as the Terms of Reference specifically require.

Deliverable

A detailed Productivity and Competitiveness chapter forming Part 2 of the final report, including the three-tier adoption framework, sector-specific opportunity analysis with realistic productivity projections, 8–10 SME case studies, international benchmarking data, and assessment of current intervention adequacy.

5. AC-3: Identifying Barriers and Enablers for SME AI Adoption (10%)

Overview

Blue Canvas AI will deliver a rigorous analysis of the barriers preventing and enablers driving SME AI adoption across the island of Ireland, benchmarked against international best practice to provide actionable, evidence-based mitigation strategies.

Methodology

3.1 Barrier and Enabler Framework (Weeks 3–4)

Barriers:

DimensionKey Issues to Investigate
Skills & TalentAI literacy gaps in SME leadership and workforce; shortage of affordable AI expertise; limited access to training; rural/urban digital divide
Finance & InvestmentCost of AI implementation relative to SME budgets; uncertainty about ROI; limited access to innovation funding; grant programme awareness
Awareness & UnderstandingLow awareness of AI applications beyond generative AI; misconceptions about complexity and cost; lack of trusted guidance; information overload
Regulation & ComplianceNavigating dual UK/EU AI regulatory frameworks; data protection concerns (GDPR in both jurisdictions); sector-specific compliance; uncertainty creating paralysis
Infrastructure & EcosystemDigital connectivity (particularly rural border areas); access to AI service providers; vendor lock-in concerns; interoperability with legacy systems

Enablers:

DimensionKey Factors
Leadership & CultureManagement digital literacy; willingness to experiment; peer learning and demonstration effects
Support EcosystemEnterprise agency programmes; university/industry partnerships; AI communities of practice; cross-border networks
Market DemandCustomer expectations; supply chain requirements; competitive pressure; public sector procurement signals
Policy EnvironmentGovernment AI strategies; regulatory clarity; innovation tax incentives (R&D tax credits in both jurisdictions); broadband investment

3.2 Primary Research — Barrier Validation

Our SME survey (200+ respondents) will include a dedicated barriers and enablers section with ranking of barriers by perceived severity (Likert scale), self-assessed digital maturity and AI readiness, awareness and uptake of existing support programmes, and cross-tabulation by jurisdiction, sector, size, and urban/rural location.

Stakeholder interviews and roundtables will probe barriers in depth — particularly the "hidden" barriers that don't appear in surveys (e.g., cultural resistance, time poverty in owner-managed SMEs, distrust of technology vendors).

3.3 International Benchmarking (Weeks 4–6)

We will benchmark the all-island position against jurisdictions recognised as leaders in SME digital/AI adoption:

  • Denmark: SME:Digital programme — government-funded digital advisors working one-to-one with SMEs; demonstrated measurable productivity gains
  • Estonia: e-Estonia digital infrastructure enabling seamless AI integration; lessons on how public digital services create readiness for private AI adoption
  • Singapore: SME Go Digital and AI Apprenticeship programmes — structured adoption pathways with co-funding
  • Scotland: CivTech programme and Scottish Enterprise AI support — directly comparable small economy with devolved administration
  • EU-wide: European Digital Innovation Hubs (EDIHs) — model for cross-border AI support delivery

For each comparator we will assess: what works, what's transferable to the all-island context, and what InterTradeIreland can uniquely deliver.

3.4 The Dual Regulatory Dimension

A unique barrier for all-island SMEs is operating under dual UK/EU regulatory regimes. We will specifically analyse:

  • EU AI Act obligations for SMEs trading into EU markets (including NI under the Windsor Framework)
  • UK's pro-innovation regulatory approach and its implications for NI-based SMEs
  • Practical compliance burden for cross-border SMEs (data flows, AI classification requirements, sector-specific rules)
  • Opportunities: how the dual-regime position could become a competitive advantage (access to both markets, regulatory arbitrage for AI development)

3.5 Evidence-Based Mitigation Strategies

For each significant barrier identified, we will propose specific, costed, and implementable mitigation strategies:

  • Skills: AI literacy programmes for SME leadership; peer-learning networks; cross-border mentoring schemes
  • Finance: Structured co-investment models; AI voucher schemes (following Denmark's model); accessible micro-grants for first AI implementations
  • Awareness: Trusted intermediary model; sector-specific AI showcases; "AI champions" network of early-adopting SMEs
  • Regulation: Plain-language compliance guides for dual-regime navigation; regulatory readiness self-assessment tools; cross-border regulatory advice service
  • Infrastructure: Leveraging existing digital infrastructure investments; cloud-first approaches reducing on-premise requirements

Key Differentiator

Blue Canvas encounters these barriers every day in our consulting work. We don't need to theorise about what stops SMEs adopting AI — we see it in practice. The most common barrier we encounter isn't skills or finance; it's time poverty and cognitive overload in owner-managed businesses. This ground-level insight, combined with rigorous international benchmarking, ensures our analysis captures both the structural and practical barriers that shape SME decision-making.

Deliverable

A comprehensive Barriers and Enablers chapter forming Part 3 of the final report, including the structured barrier/enabler framework, survey findings with cross-tabulations, international benchmarking analysis, dual-regulation deep-dive, and specific mitigation strategies for each identified barrier.

PILLAR 1: All-Island AI Knowledge and Coordination

Recommendation 1.1 — Establish an All-Island AI for SMEs Observatory

InterTradeIreland should create a lightweight, ongoing research and monitoring function that tracks AI adoption across both jurisdictions, maintains the landscape map produced by this research, and publishes annual benchmarking data.

Rationale: Current research is fragmented and jurisdiction-specific. No single body monitors all-island SME AI adoption.

InterTradeIreland's unique role: Only body with the mandate and relationships to deliver all-island data collection and analysis.

Implementation: Commission annual survey; publish All-Island AI Adoption Index; share data with DETE, DfE, and enterprise agencies.

Estimated cost: £25,000–35,000 per annum. Timeframe: Establish within 6 months.

Recommendation 1.2 — Cross-Border AI Policy Coordination Forum

Establish a biannual policy coordination forum bringing together AI policy leads from DfE, DETE, Invest NI, Enterprise Ireland, AICC, and other stakeholders to align interventions, share intelligence, and avoid duplication.

InterTradeIreland's unique role: Convening power across both jurisdictions; neutral all-island platform.

Implementation: Biannual half-day events (rotating Belfast/Dublin); published communiqué; action tracking.

Estimated cost: £8,000–12,000 per annum. Timeframe: First meeting within 3 months.

PILLAR 2: SME AI Adoption Support

Recommendation 2.1 — All-Island AI Readiness Programme for SMEs

A structured, cross-border programme providing SMEs with:

  • Stage 1 — AI Readiness Assessment: Free online self-assessment tool
  • Stage 2 — AI Discovery Workshops: Half-day sector-specific workshops across both jurisdictions
  • Stage 3 — AI Implementation Support: Matched co-funding (£5,000–£10,000) for first substantive AI projects

Implementation: Year 1 pilot with 100 SMEs (50 per jurisdiction); evaluate and scale.

Estimated programme cost: £200,000–£350,000 per annum. Timeframe: Design and pilot within 12 months.

Recommendation 2.2 — AI Peer Learning Network

Create a cross-border peer learning network connecting SMEs at similar stages of AI adoption for shared learning, problem-solving, and encouragement.

Implementation: Monthly online meetups; quarterly in-person events; curated case studies; facilitated by practitioners.

Estimated cost: £15,000–25,000 per annum. Timeframe: Launch within 6 months.

Recommendation 2.3 — All-Island AI Voucher Scheme

A cross-border voucher scheme (modelled on Denmark's SME:Digital) providing SMEs with £2,000–£5,000 to purchase AI advisory services from approved providers.

Implementation: Panel of approved AI advisors; simple application process; post-project impact reporting.

Estimated cost: £150,000–£300,000 per annum (50–100 vouchers). Timeframe: First round within 12 months.

A short, intensive programme (2–3 days) for SME decision-makers covering: what AI can and cannot do; identifying AI opportunities; evaluating AI vendors; managing implementation risk; and navigating regulatory requirements (UK and EU).
