Anti-Bullying Week 2026 planning guide for schools

Anti-Bullying Week can be more than a one-off assembly. Used well, it gives schools a visible moment to reinforce culture, refresh reporting routes, involve pupils and connect pastoral priorities to classroom practice.

Dates and campaign context

The Anti-Bullying Alliance lists Anti-Bullying Week 2026 as 16 to 20 November. For schools in Northern Ireland, the campaign can connect naturally with statutory anti-bullying policy work, Education Authority resources and pupil wellbeing priorities. For schools in the Republic of Ireland, it can support whole-school work linked to Cinealtas and the Bi Cinealta procedures.

The key SEO opportunity is to answer the practical searches that happen months before November: assembly ideas, anti-bullying lesson plans, Odd Socks Day activities, cyberbullying resources, parent letters, staff briefing support and anti-bullying workshop providers.

A useful school planning structure

Strong campaign weeks normally have three layers. The first is visibility: posters, assemblies, class starters and pupil voice activity that make the theme easy to understand. The second is depth: guided discussion around friendship, power imbalance, bystander behaviour, online harm, respect and help-seeking. The third is follow-up: staff know how incidents are recorded, pupils know who to speak to, and families know how the school responds.

HIP Psychology workshop pages should sit in the middle of that structure. They can offer age-appropriate pupil sessions, staff briefings and planning support without pretending that a workshop replaces policy, safeguarding or pastoral leadership.
