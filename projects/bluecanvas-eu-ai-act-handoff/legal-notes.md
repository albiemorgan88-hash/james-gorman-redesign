# Legal notes

## Purpose of these notes
These notes are for the page builder, copywriter, and marketer. They are there to keep the experience commercially useful without drifting into overclaiming.

This is not formal legal advice.

## Safe framing
The page should be framed as:
- a readiness self-check
- a practical triage tool
- a plain-English starting point
- a way to identify likely pressure points and next steps

The page should not be framed as:
- a legal opinion
- a formal compliance assessment
- a certification
- a definitive scope determination

## Words and phrases to prefer
Use:
- may apply
- likely relevant
- may need attention
- suggests higher-priority review
- readiness gaps
- next-step plan
- practical review

Avoid:
- you are compliant
- you are non-compliant
- this tool determines your legal status
- guaranteed in scope
- guaranteed out of scope

## Verified timing anchors
Use these exact dates, phrased as **as at April 14, 2026**:
- AI Act entered into force: **1 August 2024**
- Prohibited practices, definitions, and AI literacy provisions: **2 February 2025**
- Governance rules and GPAI obligations: **2 August 2025**
- Most remaining obligations: **2 August 2026**
- Some product safety / Annex I-linked obligations: **2 August 2027**

## Required caveat on timing
Include a short note that the European Commission proposed simplification changes on **19 November 2025**, so timing and scope messaging should avoid pretending everything is fixed beyond doubt.

Good example:
- As at April 14, 2026, most remaining obligations are due from 2 August 2026, although implementation detail and simplification proposals mean businesses should keep guidance under review.

## Practical copy guardrails
### On the page
Good:
- Check how ready your business is for the EU AI Act
- See where you may need to act
- Get a practical next-step view

Risky:
- Find out if you are compliant
- Certify your AI Act compliance
- Instantly know your legal obligations with certainty

### In result states
Good:
- high-priority review needed
- priority readiness work needed
- early action recommended

Risky:
- non-compliant
- unlawful
- approved
- certified

## Scope caution
Do not oversimplify the Act into a pure geography question. The copy should reflect that relevance can depend on:
- whether systems are placed on the EU market
- whether they are deployed in the EU
- whether outputs affect people in the EU
- the role the business plays, for example provider or deployer
- the kind of use case involved

## Prohibited-practice caution
If the checker surfaces a possible prohibited-practice issue, the result copy should:
- stay careful
- avoid making a legal accusation
- encourage prompt review

Recommended wording:
- Your answers suggest a use case that may need urgent review under the EU AI Act.

## Annex I / product safety caution
Where product safety or Annex I-linked obligations are mentioned, keep them high level unless legal has approved more detailed wording.

Recommended phrasing:
- Some product safety and Annex I-linked obligations are due from 2 August 2027.

## Footer or microcopy recommendation
Use a short disclaimer close to the checker and form:
- This self-check is for general information and readiness planning. It is not formal legal advice or a substitute for matter-specific review.

## Internal recommendation
If Blue Canvas follows up on stronger-risk leads, the outreach should position the next step as:
- a practical scoping call
- a readiness workshop
- a governance and documentation review

That keeps the offer commercial and useful without implying regulated legal services.
