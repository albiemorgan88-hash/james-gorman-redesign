## 1. 🔍 GOOGLE ADS BAN — IS IT STILL ACTIVE?

### CONFIRMED: The Botox/POM advertising ban is STILL ACTIVE in 2026 ✅

**What happened in September 2025:** Google removed restrictions on "Mature Cosmetic Procedures" — but this was specifically about **intimate body part surgeries** (breast augmentation, penis enlargement, vaginal rejuvenation, buttock implants). This has **NOTHING to do with Botox**.

**Why Botox remains banned:** The restriction on Botox advertising is NOT a Google policy choice — it's **UK law**. Botulinum toxin is a Prescription Only Medicine (POM). Under the Human Medicines Regulations 2012 and enforced by the ASA via CAP Code rule 12.12, it is **illegal** to advertise POMs to the public. This isn't going away unless UK pharmaceutical law changes — extremely unlikely.

**Proof it's still active (2025-2026):**
- June 2025: Reddit r/PPC user reports Google Ads **instantly classifying entire domain as "prescription drug"** because of a single Botox page — even campaigns pointing to other URLs get refused
- April 2025: Dental Design Marketing confirms clinics cannot use "Botox" or "Botulinum Toxin" in Google Ads
- Oct 2025: Search Engine Land reports Google actually *loosened* some prescription drug term restrictions for non-promotional use — but clinics still getting ads approved and disapproved inconsistently. The word "Botox" on your site = entire domain flagged
- 2025: ASA now using **custom AI technology** to proactively detect non-compliance with POM advertising rules

**Workarounds clinics use:**
- Advertising "anti-wrinkle injections" or "facial aesthetics" instead of Botox
- Creating separate landing pages/domains that never mention Botox for paid traffic
- But even these are risky — Dec 2024 ASA ruling (Valterous Ltd) found that separating "cosmetic injections" from "dermal fillers" was STILL indirect POM promotion

**Impact on thesis:** The moat is STRONGER than we initially thought. It's not just Google policy — it's UK pharmaceutical law. The ASA is getting stricter, not looser, and using AI to enforce. Clinics face a genuine nightmare trying to run paid ads.

## 8. 🤔 THE HONEST QUESTION — Is This As Good As It Looks?

### What we might be missing:

**1. The sales problem.** Ranking a site is the EASY part. Convincing a clinic owner to pay you £500-1,000/month for leads requires sales skills. Aesthetics practitioners are busy medical professionals, not business people browsing for marketing solutions. You need to physically walk into clinics or call them. PJ can do this — he's in Belfast and he's a good communicator. But it IS a step many rank & renters skip.

**2. Why hasn't anyone done it already?** Actually, they HAVE — lipfillersbelfast.co.uk was registered in Sep 2024. But rank & rent in general is niche knowledge. Most SEOs work for agencies or do client work. The Venn diagram of "knows SEO" + "knows about the Botox ad ban" + "lives in Belfast/cares about NI market" is very small. **PJ is in that Venn diagram.**

**3. The ASA is getting stricter.** The 2024 Valterous ruling expanded what counts as indirect POM promotion. A rank & rent site that is clearly a lead generation front might attract ASA attention if it mentions Botox prominently. This is manageable with careful content, but it's a real constraint.

**4. Trust signals.** Aesthetics is a trust-sensitive industry. People want to know WHO is treating them. A generic "Aesthetics Belfast" site without a real practitioner might feel untrustworthy to consumers. Solution: once you have a clinic partner, add their credentials and reviews to the site.

**5. Google Business Profile.** Local pack rankings (the map results) are huge for local searches. You can't create a GBP without a real business address. This limits your ranking potential vs actual clinics that have GBP listings. You'll be competing for the organic results below the map pack.

**6. The revenue ceiling.** £500-1,000/month per site is good but not life-changing for a single city. The real money is in scaling to 5-10+ cities. That requires replicating the effort each time.

### The honest assessment:
This is a **genuinely good opportunity** but it's not a get-rich-quick scheme. It requires:
- 3-5 months of patience before any revenue
- Sales effort to find clinic partners
- Careful compliance with ASA/medical advertising rules
- Scaling discipline to replicate across cities

The structural advantage (POM ad ban) is REAL and DURABLE. The competition is genuinely weak. The economics work. But execution matters.
