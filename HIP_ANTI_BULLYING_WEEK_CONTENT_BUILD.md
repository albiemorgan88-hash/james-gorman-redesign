Heading:
A focused week needs more than posters

Body:
Anti-Bullying Week is a useful moment to start conversations, but schools need more than a once-a-year message. Pupils need language they understand, staff need shared confidence, and parents need to know how school and home can respond consistently.

HIP Psychology workshops are designed to make the week practical. We help schools move from awareness to action through clear discussion, age-appropriate examples and realistic strategies pupils can use in school, online and at home.

## Section 3 - Topics Covered

Heading:
Topics schools are dealing with now

Intro:
Every session is adapted to the age and needs of the group. Common themes include:

Topic list:
- What bullying behaviour is and how it differs from conflict or falling out
- The role of repetition, intent, impact and power imbalance
- Cyberbullying, group chats and online pile-ons
- Bystander behaviour and safe ways to speak up
- Friendship issues, exclusion and social pressure
- Respect, empathy and school culture
- How pupils can report concerns and ask for help
- The impact of false accusations and careless language
- Supporting pupils who have been harmed
- Helping pupils repair harm and change behaviour

## Section 4 - Age-Specific Delivery

Heading:
Age-appropriate, not one-size-fits-all

Primary copy:
Younger pupils need simple language, concrete examples and clear routes to help. Sessions focus on kindness, friendship, inclusion, naming worries and understanding how actions affect others.

Post-primary / secondary copy:
Older pupils need honest discussion about social pressure, group chats, screenshots, reputation, exclusion, bystander choices and the difference between banter, conflict and bullying behaviour.

Staff copy:
Staff sessions support a consistent whole-school approach, helping adults use shared language and follow up beyond Anti-Bullying Week.

## Internal Linking Rules

- Every SEO support page links to /anti-bullying-week-workshops/ with "Check workshop availability".
- The main landing page links to NI, Ireland, cyberbullying, assemblies and planning checklist pages.
- Blog/resource pages should answer the search query first, then offer workshops naturally.
- Do not use phrases like "SEO hub", "content engine", "linkable resource" or "keyword strategy" on public-facing pages.

## Public Copy Rules

- Talk to teachers and school leaders, not marketers.
- Use "bullying behaviour" where appropriate, especially in NI context.
- Avoid overclaiming. Do not promise to stop bullying.
- Avoid shaming language such as "bully" and "victim" where more careful wording works.
- Keep the tone practical, calm and school-aware.
- Make every section answer: "Why would a school care?"
